ad hoc hand-written Java and jQuery, versioned 1.0 → 1.2 through fall 2023. The AI was the feature, not the author.

The pitch that won AEM Rockstar at Adobe Summit 2024: personalization at scale, inside AEM's own authoring UI. An author opens an experience fragment, types "rewrite this for ages 13 to 17," reviews a side-by-side table of every proposed change, and one click later has a new fragment variation open in the editor.

The design constraint that shaped it: add capability without adding surface. Generation is a Sling selector on the stock experience-fragment page type, the UI is one clientlib injected into the stock properties editor, and writes go through AEM's own copy and POST machinery. No new endpoints, no new editor, no writes without review.

May 2023 · where it started

AEM has content

I came back from paternity leave really interested in generative AI, and back at work I was the AEM architect doing a lot of business development. That means telling stories about past implementations, and I'm not very good at it: I'd say I spent 98% of my time building solutions and less than 2% telling compelling stories about them.

So I worked with ChatGPT until it gave me the story I was looking for. The secret sauce: paste in the case study, a little about the client you're presenting to, and something the slide doesn't know, like a struggle in the middle or a few dates. It wasn't perfect right away, but it was way better than anything I could have come up with.

That was my generative AI moment, and right behind it came the epiphany: AEM has content. Generative AI is very good at analyzing content, transforming content and generating new content from what you have. The framework does four things, but really it's two and two: you choose the AI model, for legal or content reasons, and it's built into AEM, so it finds the content for you and saves it right back. Nobody pastes anything.

Mar 28, 2024 · demo 1, personalization at scale

A personalized variation in one click

The demo starts with an experience fragment, the Adobe Summit 2024 sign up banner, rebuilt on a WKND instance. I took Summit's imagery but didn't quite get the font, so, Adobe judges, please don't be too harsh. The font isn't brand approved, but the text is.

In the fragment's properties there's an extra tab, AI Personalization. I type an audience, say banking aem customer, and the console shows every text property it found, with its original and generated value. If I like what I see, I name the variation and click Create Variation, and a few seconds later the new variation is open in the editor, saved.

On stage I used diamond partners. It's like Marriott telling me I'm Platinum every time I book: tell your diamond partners they're diamond, and they feel good about it. And why experience fragments? You can export them to Target, to Campaign, pretty much anywhere. They're the perfect medium between the page and the content fragment, so to me, personalization at scale starts with experience fragments.

One Generate Content click: each text property with its original and generated value. From my own recording of the demo, two days before Summit.

Process version
By hand
  1. Author copies the fragment's heading and body text out of AEM
  2. Author pastes them into a chat window, with the audience and a prompt
  3. AI rewrites the copy
  4. Author copies each rewritten value back
  5. Author creates a new variation
  6. Author pastes each value into its field and saves
Five of the six steps are the author's, and the content leaves AEM and comes back by hand.
With the framework
  1. Author types the audience: banking aem customer
  2. AEM finds every text and description property in the variation
  3. AI rewrites each one, inside a prompt template the author never sees
  4. Author reviews the original and generated value of each row, and types a name for the variation
  5. AEM copies the variation
  6. AEM saves the generated values and opens the new variation in the editor
The author types once and reviews once. AEM finds the content and saves it, and nothing is pasted anywhere.

The whole flow. Drawn for this page from the repo's code.

The frontend is where most of the craft went. A ~270-line Granite/jQuery clientlib hooks foundation-contentloaded, derives the fragment path from the properties-editor URL, and builds the review table in the DOM: path, original value, generated value, one row per property the QueryBuilder walk found. A continue-chat toggle threads the chat id back, so the model refines its last answer instead of starting over. Approval clones the variation through /bin/wcmcommand, writes the accepted values with a Sling POST, and links straight into the new fragment's editor. A second clientlib does the same for whole pages.

None of this code was AI-written; in August 2023 the AI was the product, GPT-4 by default with the model behind a config. The shape it demoed is the one everything above formalizes later as propose and dispose: the model proposes values, a human reviews every row, and deterministic machinery does the writing. In 2023 that pattern was a jQuery table.

01d6c49 · under the console

One selector servlet, one query, two writes

The servlet is bound to the stock experience fragment page type with its own selector, so there's no new page type and no new path: it answers on the variation's own URL, and the author's permissions on that fragment apply. The prompt the author types is only part of what the model gets; the service wraps it in a template the author never sees.

Mar 28, 2024 · demo 2, content summarization

An SEO title and description from the page itself

On stage, this is where the venue internet got me: the page properties wouldn't open. I said I had a recording, talked it through, and moved on to fine-tuning. My own recording from two days earlier shows it working.

Same console idea, except the prompt is in the back: it asks for an SEO friendly title and an SEO friendly description. On a WKND adventure page, two checkboxes, and Save Checked Content writes the page's jcr:title and jcr:description.

To summarize a page the model needs all of it, so the service renders the page's content tree as JSON inside AEM, through the Sling request processor, with no HTTP round trip (getPageContent). And this is just a start: with the right prompt it can tag things, check for plagiarism or check spelling.

The AI Content Refinement console: a title and a description generated from the page's own content.

Mar 28, 2024 · demo 3, fine-tuning

A fine-tuned model, switched in from OSGi

Can we do better than the generic models? The answer is absolutely yes. The model is a field in the framework's OSGi configuration, next to the secret key and the max tokens: paste in the fine-tuned model's id, save, and every call after that goes to it.

Integrating the model takes a few hundred lines of code. The fine-tuning is where the data science is. A friend and I spent our proof of concept on one question: how do we get summer to do summer really well? All 30 training examples use the prompt summer, and each one carries the exact template from the console.

That's what I answered in the Q&A. In my runs the general models failed about 2 to 3% of the time, not following the prompt, and the fine-tuned model succeeded every time, because it's fed the same prompt it was trained on. And for several products on one AEM instance: a fine-tuned model can hold 20 different systems, and as long as you call it with the exact system you trained with, it works. System for system is how the capabilities stay apart.

The OSGi configuration with a fine-tuned gpt-3.5-turbo-1106 model in place of the default gpt-4. The rest of the model id is blurred.

Mar 28, 2024 · my favorite slide

Three ways to train a content model

First, existing data: SEO descriptions or personalized content you're already happy with, fed right in.

Second, the human in the loop: people refine what the model generates, and the refined data makes the model better. That's how we get to the segment of one.

Third, an idea, not part of the framework code: capture the best output generative AI produces in our consoles, and the corrections authors make, and feed that back into the model.

My slide on customer trained models: existing data, a human in the loop, and corrections sent back from AEM.

01d6c49 · what works, and what does not

The limits I would fix first

What works end to end: a fragment variation from a prompt, with a review table and a one click save; an SEO title and description saved to the page; any OpenAI chat model, fine-tuned ones included, set by configuration. What I didn't build: the feedback loop, images, export to Target from the console, and any measured evaluation. The 2 to 3% is what I saw in my runs, not a benchmark.

Read the code and three limits show. The Continue Chat history lives in memory in one OSGi service, so it's per author instance and gone on a restart (line 50). Every text property is its own model call. And the Sling POST body is built by joining strings without URL encoding, so a generated value with an & or = in it would break the save (line 228).

What changed after AEM Rockstar 2024
Since then

Adobe ships the pattern

Generate Variations in AEM as a Cloud Service writes copy variations from prompts, in the navigation panel, the Sidekick or the Content Fragments editor, and a person picks what to use.

The models are gone

OpenAI shut down gpt-4, the default here, on October 23, 2026. The model is an OSGi setting, so that's a config change, but a fine-tuned model would need training again.

The review table stayed

The model proposes, the author reviews every row, and deterministic code writes. I build that way now, with agents (From Prompts to Harness).

If you've put generative AI inside AEM authoring for real authors, I'd like to compare notes: did they trust a review table, or want something else?
